The argument in one picture

Moving short feeder flights onto high-speed rail looks like a clear climate gain. At a congested hub, though, every feeder flight occupies a runway slot. Once the flight moves to rail, the slot is free, and the airline can use it again, often for a longer and more profitable route.

Dobruszkes studies Frankfurt Airport ex post, over a period in which airport capacity stayed stable. The paper finds that air/HSR integration did not reduce aviation's climate impact there: freed slots were reused, and on balance the impact rose. Integration is not a climate fix on its own. Its effect depends on what happens to the slots.

The toy model lets you test this. With 0 % slot reuse the substitution saves CO₂. As reuse and stage length grow, the balance tips. The readout gives the reuse share at which the gain disappears.

Simplifications: generic hub, made-up feeder distances, a two-class linear emission model per flight (assumptions above, editable). Rail emissions are a flat value per train. These are not figures from the paper, which works with observed Frankfurt traffic. Use the paper for the actual numbers.
