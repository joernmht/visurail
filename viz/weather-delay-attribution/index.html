Why this split matters

A train that is 12 minutes late at a station may have lost only 1 minute there. The other 11 it brought along. If you want to learn how weather causes delay, the total delay is a poor target. It mixes the weather here with everything that happened earlier.

FI-TW joins Finnish train-running records (Digitraffic) with nearby weather observations (Finnish Meteorological Institute) for 2018–2024. Its baseline model predicts a station-specific delay with the propagated part removed. In the diagram that is the orange part of each bar. The blue part is what the train inherited.

Try it. Raise the snow level and the orange parts grow everywhere. Click a station to add an incident: it creates a large orange spike there, and the blue part then carries it down the line. A running-time supplement lets the train win some of it back on each section.

Simplifications: one train, eight made-up stations, a linear toy rule (snow adds dwell and running time; the supplement is recovered when late). No numbers on this page come from the dataset. A next version should read real train runs from FI-TW (see issues).
