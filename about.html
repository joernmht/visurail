About visurail

visurail collects interactive visualizations of rail science. Each one takes a single idea from an open-access paper and turns it into something you can try in the browser: a slider, a diagram, a small model. The paper stays the authority. The visualization is the way in.

Credit: Visual Transportation Science

The format is not ours. Visual Transportation Science pioneered pairing transportation research with interactive visualizations. visurail applies that idea to one field, rail, so that the catalog can be structured more tightly. No code or content from Visual Transportation Science is used here. If you like this site, go and look at theirs.

How the catalog is structured

Within rail, the field divides cleanly, so the catalog uses two axes instead of one long topic list:

  • Rail domains: where in the railway system the work sits. These are Operations, Infrastructure & Construction, Signalling & Control, Vehicles & Dynamics, Network Planning & Economics, Energy & Environment, and Safety & Reliability. Each work has one primary domain and may have secondary ones.
  • Interfaces: where rail meets another system. These are Rail × Air, Rail × Road, Rail × Water, Rail × Urban mobility, and Rail × Weather & Climate. A work can sit on several.

Facets for segment (passenger, freight, …) and method (optimisation, simulation, network science, …) cut across both axes. The taxonomy is a proposal, and we expect it to change once more works come in.
